INDIA INCOME TAX — OLD VS NEW REGIME COMPARISON FY 2026-27
===========================================================

SOURCE: Finance Act 2025, Income Tax Act 1961 as amended

1. NEW TAX REGIME (Default from FY 2023-24 onwards)
------------------------------------------------------
The New Tax Regime is the DEFAULT regime. Taxpayers must explicitly opt OUT to use Old Regime.

New Regime Tax Slabs FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28):
  Up to ₹4,00,000           — NIL
  ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000    — 5%
  ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000   — 10%
  ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000  — 15%
  ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000  — 20%
  ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000  — 25%
  Above ₹24,00,000           — 30%

Key features of New Regime:
  - Standard Deduction: ₹75,000 (for salaried/pensioners)
  - Section 87A rebate: Zero tax if income up to ₹12,00,000 (after standard deduction)
    i.e., gross salary up to ₹12,75,000 → ZERO tax under new regime
  - NO deductions allowed: 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B), HRA, LTA, home loan interest
  - Employer NPS contribution (80CCD(2)) IS allowed — up to 14% of basic salary
  - Family pension standard deduction: ₹25,000
  - Transport allowance for specially abled: allowed

2. OLD TAX REGIME
------------------
Old Regime Tax Slabs FY 2026-27:
  Up to ₹2,50,000            — NIL
  ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000    — 5%
  ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000   — 20%
  Above ₹10,00,000            — 30%

  Senior Citizens (60-80 years): NIL up to ₹3,00,000
  Super Senior Citizens (80+):   NIL up to ₹5,00,000

Key features of Old Regime:
  - Standard Deduction: ₹50,000
  - ALL deductions available: 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, home loan, 80CCD(1B), etc.
  - Section 87A rebate: ₹12,500 if taxable income ≤ ₹5,00,000
  - Must be explicitly chosen each year (for non-business taxpayers)

3. SURCHARGE (both regimes)
-----------------------------
  ₹50L to ₹1Cr:    10%
  ₹1Cr to ₹2Cr:    15%
  ₹2Cr to ₹5Cr:    25%
  Above ₹5Cr:       37% (new regime) / 37% (old regime)
  Note: 25% surcharge cap applies on LTCG/STCG/dividend under both regimes

4. HEALTH AND EDUCATION CESS: 4% on (tax + surcharge)

5. WHEN NEW REGIME IS BETTER (typically):
  - Income below ₹12.75L (zero tax)
  - Low investments/deductions (under ₹1.75L 80C+80D+HRA combined)
  - No home loan
  - Young earners with fewer deductions

6. WHEN OLD REGIME IS BETTER (typically):
  - High 80C investments (₹1.5L fully utilized)
  - High HRA exemption (metro cities)
  - Home loan interest (₹2L Section 24b)
  - 80D health insurance (₹25K-₹75K)
  - NPS 80CCD(1B) ₹50K additional
  - Combined deductions exceed ₹3.75L

7. BREAK-EVEN ANALYSIS:
  The old regime becomes better when total deductions exceed approximately:
    ₹3,75,000 at ₹15L income
    ₹4,25,000 at ₹20L income
    ₹5,00,000 at ₹25L income

8. SWITCHING RULES:
  - Salaried: Can switch every year when filing ITR
  - Business income: Once switched to new regime, cannot go back to old regime
  - Employer: Employee must inform employer of regime choice at start of FY for TDS purposes
